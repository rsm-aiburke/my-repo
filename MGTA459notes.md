## Major Contributors to Overconfidence

### Myopic Individual Perspectives

People tend to focus on the things they know, it's hard to focus on things that are not in direct view of oneself

No one can really see the big picture in total, we can try to think about it as a concept but it's hard to really grasp it

### Biased Construal

People will spin things based on what they think

Think of the little girl taking a test, people will have expectations coming in and those watching the video will think about it one way or another based on their expectations

### Prediction by Evaluation

Taking some of what we know to predict something, we should be using everything we know even if they might not feel directly applicable

Prediction has to be more moderate than the evaluation, closer to the average

# Lecture 3

## Averaging Difference

Green Triangle difference

- gives everyone an equal voice and are respected equally
- If one person dominates a conversation/decision then that opinion may take over more when the decisions come together

## Independent Perspectives = An Asset

Putting people together who think about things differently will come out with a better result than just the high performer alone

Having a low performer and high performer together can perform better than two high performers because the low performer may think differently and think of problems in the high performers process that the he/she did not think of the first time

## Chasing the Expert

One contributor to this pitfall is that confidence is taken as a sign of accuracy when there is only a weak correlation between the two

## Organizational Priorities

Default: recruiting and retaining people who fit in

Also needed: facilitating, maintaining and leveraging people's independent perspective

## Fundamental Tradeoff

Ensuring informational diversity typically means sacrificing social cohesion and vice versa

### Tradeoff Across time

Information capability often important early: discovery, judgement, problem solving

Cohesion is better later: buy-in, action, execution

Diverge then converge

## Optimizing Idea Generation

### Two methods

Brainstorming

- work as a group
- Ground rule: no judgement

Nominal Group Technique (NGT)

- Each person brainstorms alone
- Also no judgement
- Won't be as influenced by the ideas by others, might start to think ideas along their thoughts
- This tends to give better ideas and more ideas

## Sequence to Optimize Group work

Initially work alone then work together

maximize chances of reaching green triangle, then try to beat the green triangle

## Taking Risks

People are reluctant to take shots on one shot games but put it into multiple tries then they are more likely to accept

## Risk and Gains

Risk averse: sure of gaining something

Risk seeking: take the gamble of the sure thing

Mix: Change as amount changes

People are risk seeking when dealing in losses, Switch the winnings for loses in these scenarios

## Ref-dep and narrow framing

Feelings oof being ahead vs. behind or whether a given outcome is a gain or loss arise relative to some salient standard

## Loss Aversion

People are more worried about the losses than they are about winning

Around 2x is where it might lean one way or another

## Business Strategy for taking risk

Aggregation is the antidote for excess risk aversion

to achieve aggregation, try to form decisions via general policies not reactions to a given situation

general policies help reduce susceptibility to vagaries of specific narrow frames

## Networks

### Human Capital vs social capital

- Human Capital Differences
  - people cary in intellect and knowledge
  - like being smart or better educated
- Social capital differences
  - people vary in ability to coordinate and add value with and through others
  - your network is a key component of social capital
- Parallels
  - build it over time
  - vital contributor to how you perform at work
  - a reason firms hire you

## underappreciated network factor

- structural holes: position that bridges people who are not otherwise connected
- Dense vs disconnected networks
  - people all know each other within a group in a dense network
  - there are sub groups but are connected through a single person
- If you remove the red dot
  - Connected: everything stays fairly similar because everyone knew each other
  - Disconnected: groups get cut off if the connecting person is gone (creates structural hole)
- Being in areas that people frequently go to
  - An office by the bathroom, an apartment by the trash
- Look to bridge the gaps between departments

## Cultivating a network

- network can't be "built" to serve a purpose
  - takes time
  - need to avoid taking rather than giving
  - Best as a way of life
- Be reliable
  - performance and consistency
- put time into relationships
  - dont under do it
- lean on reciprocity

## Reciprocity vs. transactions

- deeply ingrained ot treat others the way they treat us
  - generous to those who are generous, ie
- value of controlling resources depends on how you control them
  - obvious: make trades
  - no so obvious: generosity, unrequested favors

### Reciprocity

- more emotion laden than exchange

## Common network mistakes

- waiting to network until you have an objective
  - should be way of life rather than means to an end
- focusing on networking up
- spending too much time with birds of a same feather
